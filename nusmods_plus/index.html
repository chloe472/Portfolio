NUSMods+

NUSMods+ offers a real-time collaborative workspace that allows students to visualise, propose, and confirm tutorial slots together. This extends individual timetable planning into a shared coordination for friends during course registration.

Timeline

2026
(4 months)

Role

UX Designer

Team

4 members

Methods

User interviews, wireframing, usability testing, heuristic evaluation

Target Audience

We interviewed four Year 1 to Year 4 NUS undergraduates who coordinate classes with friends during course registration planning. The research focused on small friend groups of two or more students who share overlapping modules and actively try to secure common tutorial slots before and during bidding.

“Someone will say.. Ohh, I actually cannot do this lot, then the six of us have to list out, like, I can do Class A, Class B, Class C … it's just like a lot of effort to do that” - Year 1, 2026

Problem

NUS students lack a shared, real-time workspace for collaborative timetable exploration during the course registration planning phase.

NUSMods

  • Good for individual timetable planning.
  • Not designed for real-time group negotiation.

Google Calendar

  • Useful once schedules are confirmed.
  • Troublesome for testing tutorial combinations.

Telegram

  • Fast for coordinating through group chats.
  • Difficult to compare visually.

Solution

NUSMods+ acts as a collaborative workspace where students can share, compare, and jointly edit timetables.

Wireframes

We created multi-fidelity wireframes for the prototype. I took inspiration from Google Docs and Google Calendar, already popular collaborative tools amongst students.

Testing

We conducted usability testing with four NUS undergraduates who regularly coordinate timetables with friends: three Year 2 students and one Year 4 student. Participants completed four core tasks while following a think-aloud protocol.

  • Compare your schedule with a friend and view both timetables together.
  • Join a friend's session and view both timetables together.
  • Propose having the same tutorial slot, T02, as your friend.
  • Accept or decline a slot proposal from a friend.

Outcome

30–60 min saved on average when coordinating timetables
50% reduction in coordination mistakes

Next Steps & Lessons

Next Iteration

Design a less confusing entry point for comparing timetables with friends.

Improvements

Implement proposed heuristic fixes, including proposal filters, optional reasons, and in-platform alerts.

Lessons Learned

Test assumptions early, listen for user problems over user-prescribed solutions, and validate before building too far.
